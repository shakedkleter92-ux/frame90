# 90'FRAME

## Brand Identity, Creative Direction & Visual Production

### Master Project Brief

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 01. Project Overview

**Project:** 90'FRAME\
**Product:** Digital camera application\
**Application:** https://shakedkleter92-ux.github.io/frame90/frame.html

90'FRAME is a digital camera application inspired by late-1990s and
early-2000s digital photography. It brings back the visual language,
nostalgia, imperfections, and atmosphere of early digital cameras.

The brand should feel like a digital camera operating system from 1997,
combining retro computer technology with contemporary photography and
authentic human experiences.

The project includes: - Brand identity development - Visual direction -
Social media branding - Instagram carousel design - Photography
generation - Digital camera effect demonstrations - Pixel transition
compositions - App promotion and marketing visuals

The goal is to create a recognizable and consistent visual identity
across all promotional materials.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 02. Core Creative Concept: The System Meets the Memories

The visual identity combines two contrasting worlds.

### World A --- The System

Inspired by 1990s computer interfaces, digital camera operating systems,
BIOS screens, old terminals, and computer hardware.

Visual characteristics: - Black backgrounds - Phosphor-green
typography - Pixel fonts and monospace typography - Computer boot
sequences - Digital camera icons - System messages and loading
indicators - Data readouts - Digital noise and pixelated graphics -
Old-school computer interfaces

### World B --- The Memories

Authentic photographs documenting real human experiences.

Visual characteristics: - Friends at parties - Couples and romantic
moments - Road trips and vacations - Summer experiences and nightlife -
Spontaneous and everyday moments - Direct-flash photography - Natural
lighting and warm colors - Imperfect framing and candid compositions

### The Connection

The central idea is the transition between contemporary digital
photography and the visual quality of old digital cameras.

The photographs represent memories. The interface represents the
technology used to capture and preserve them.

**Important brand rule:** The technological interface should remain
primarily black and phosphor green. The photographs should remain
colorful, warm, and realistic. Do not turn the entire photographic world
into green CCTV imagery. The green monochromatic aesthetic belongs
mainly to the interface and selected photographic treatments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 03. Visual References

Two moodboards define the visual direction.

### Moodboard A --- Technology & Interface

References include: - CCTV surveillance screens and multiple-camera
monitor layouts - Green night-vision imagery - Old computer interfaces -
Digital glitches and pixel distortion - Low-resolution digital imagery -
Data corruption and screen interference

These references should inspire the graphic language, not necessarily
the photographic content.

**Avoid making the brand look like a security company.** The intention
is digital nostalgia, not surveillance or crime aesthetics.

### Moodboard B --- Photography & Human Experiences

References include: - Friends spending time together - Couples
embracing - People at parties - Road trips and summer vacations - Candid
portraits and spontaneous moments - Warm natural lighting and editorial
lifestyle photography

Photographs should feel authentic, personal, and emotionally engaging.
Avoid generic stock photography, overly staged poses, and commercial
advertising aesthetics. Images should feel like personal photographs
taken by friends.

The primary palette is intentionally limited.

  Dark Green       `#103D25`             Secondary interface elements
  Light Green      `#A5FFB9`             Readable interface typography
  Electric Blue    Accent only           Digital glitches and pixel transitions
  REC Red          Accent only           Small recording indicators

### Color rules

1.  Black and green dominate the interface.
2.  Green glow should be subtle and controlled.
3.  Blue is an occasional accent, not a primary color.
4.  Red should appear sparingly.
5.  Photography should retain its original colors.
6.  Avoid excessive neon effects.

## 05. Typography

Use two main typographic directions.

### A. Pixel / Monospace Typography

Use for: - System messages - Camera information and timestamps - Loading
screens and file names - Technical labels and interface elements - Small
annotations

The typography should resemble old computer terminals and 1990s
operating systems.

### B. Clean Sans Serif Typography

Use for: - Marketing headlines - Explanatory text - Social media copy -
Editorial layouts - Supporting descriptions

The combination should feel like a contemporary design studio
interpreting an old computer interface. Avoid futuristic sci-fi
typography. The aesthetic should feel retro, authentic, and functional.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 06. Recurring Graphic Elements

Develop a consistent library of graphic elements.

**Camera elements** - Pixel camera icon - Camera frame corners and
viewfinder - REC indicator - Camera identification numbers - Image
resolution labels

**System elements** - BIOS screens - Memory-test messages - Loading bars
and blinking cursors - Terminal text - File information and system
status messages

**Photographic elements** - Thin image borders - Timestamp overlays -
Camera labels and image identification numbers - Multiple-camera grids -
Image archive layouts

**Digital effects** - Pixelation - Digital noise and scanlines -
Compression artifacts - RGB distortion - Glitch and pixel displacement -
Mosaic transitions

Use these elements selectively. Not every composition needs every
graphic element. The objective is consistency without visual repetition.

## 07. Signature Visual Effect: Pixel Transition

This is the most important visual feature of the campaign.

The central idea is to show the same photograph in two different digital
qualities simultaneously.

### The Two Image States

**State A --- Modern Digital Photograph** - Sharp and high resolution -
Natural colors - Contemporary digital photography - Clean details

**State B --- 90s Digital Camera** - Lower resolution - Visible digital
noise - Early digital-camera color rendering - Reduced detail - Slightly
imperfect exposure - Digital compression artifacts - Authentic retro
digital photography appearance

**Critical requirement:** Both image states must represent the exact
same photograph. The subject, composition, people, positions, clothing,
lighting, and background must remain identical.

Do not generate two separate photographs and present them as before and
after. The retro version must be derived from the original image.

### Pixel Grid Transition

Divide the photograph into a regular square grid. Some squares display
the original modern photograph; other squares display the retro camera
version. This creates a checkerboard-like transition between two
photographic qualities.

Initial grid experiments: - 8 × 8 - 10 × 10 - 12 × 12

The squares should be large enough to make the visual transition
immediately recognizable. The grid should remain geometrically
consistent.

Experiment with: - Alternating squares - Randomized square selection -
Progressive pixel transitions - Partial image degradation - Digital
displacement - Blue glitch fragments

The transition should look intentional and designed, not like random
image corruption.

### Production Requirement

1.  Generate or obtain the original photograph first.
2.  Create the retro version from the same source image.
3.  Construct the pixel grid using image processing, SVG, CSS, or
    another precise compositing method.
4.  Do not rely on AI image generation alone to create an accurate
    checkerboard transition.

Precision and consistency are essential.

## 08. Photography Direction

Build a library of original photographs for the campaign. All images
should share a consistent photographic language.

### Category 01 --- Party

**Subjects:** Friends dancing, house parties, nightclubs, friends
laughing, direct-flash moments, crowded rooms, spontaneous nightlife.

**Visual direction:** Direct flash, dark backgrounds, high contrast,
natural movement, slightly imperfect framing.

### Category 02 --- Friends

**Subjects:** Groups of friends, road trips, sitting together, laughing,
walking through the city, hanging out outdoors.

**Visual direction:** Candid photography, warm colors, natural light,
casual compositions.

### Category 03 --- Love

**Subjects:** Couples embracing, kissing, walking together, romantic
summer moments, spontaneous interactions.

**Visual direction:** Golden hour, warm sunlight, cinematic atmosphere,
natural body language.

### Category 04 --- Travel

**Subjects:** Road trips, beaches, mountains, summer vacations, friends
exploring new places, car windows and landscapes.

**Visual direction:** Nostalgic travel photography, natural colors,
slightly imperfect compositions, authentic experiences.

### Category 05 --- Everyday

**Subjects:** Everyday moments, friends at home, coffee shops, street
photography, small personal experiences.

**Visual direction:** Documentary photography, unposed subjects, natural
lighting, personal and intimate atmosphere.

## 09. Photography Generation Rules

When generating photographs: 1. Prioritize photorealism. 2. Use
authentic human interactions. 3. Avoid artificial-looking faces. 4.
Avoid overly polished commercial photography. 5. Use spontaneous
compositions. 6. Include environmental context. 7. Use natural body
language. 8. Create varied framing and camera angles. 9. Maintain visual
consistency across the library. 10. Avoid text, logos, and watermarks
inside generated photographs.

Images should feel like real moments captured by someone who was there.

**Image consistency rule:** When creating modern and retro versions of
an image, always use the same original photograph as the source.
Preserve the original composition exactly.

transition effect.

the application.

A black screen with phosphor-green terminal typography.

Suggested copy:

**OPEN 90'FRAME**

Include the actual application link.

## 11. Composition System

The social media identity should combine three visual composition types.

### Type A --- System Screen

Black background, green pixel typography, system messages, loading
screens, icons, and computer-interface elements.

### Type B --- Memory Archive

Photographic collages, multiple image sizes, thin borders, camera
labels, timestamps, and editorial layouts.

### Type C --- Pixel Transition

Large photographic compositions, visible checkerboard transitions,
modern and retro image qualities, and digital distortion.

Alternate these three composition types throughout the campaign. Avoid
making every slide look identical.

## 13. Creative Quality Control

Evaluate every new visual using these questions:

1.  Does it feel connected to the 1990s digital-camera aesthetic?
2.  Does it preserve the contrast between technology and human memories?
3.  Is the photography authentic and emotionally engaging?
4.  Is the black and phosphor-green interface language consistent?
5.  Is the pixel transition visually understandable?
6.  Does the composition feel like a designed editorial image rather
    than generic AI output?
7.  Does it contribute something new to the overall campaign?

If an image looks too futuristic, too cyberpunk, too much like
surveillance footage, or too much like generic stock photography,
reconsider the direction.

The brand should feel nostalgic rather than futuristic.

## 14. Working Instructions for the AI Agent

Act as a senior creative director, brand designer, art director, and
visual production assistant.

Your responsibility is to help develop and maintain the complete visual
identity of 90'FRAME.

### Creative Direction

-   Maintain the established brand identity.
-   Respect the existing application interface.
-   Prioritize visual consistency.
-   Suggest creative improvements when relevant.
-   Challenge weak visual ideas.
-   Avoid generic design solutions.
-   Think in terms of a complete visual system, not isolated images.

### Image Production

-   Develop detailed image-generation prompts.
-   Maintain consistency between image variations.
-   Preserve source-image composition when generating effects.
-   Separate photography generation from graphic compositing.
-   Use precise pixel grids for transitions.
-   Prefer controlled post-processing over unreliable AI-generated text
    or geometry.

### Technical Work

1.  Inspect the existing project structure before modifying files.
2.  Understand the current implementation.
3.  Preserve existing functionality.
4.  Avoid unnecessary code changes.
5.  Do not overwrite assets without permission.
6.  Keep generated assets organized.
7.  Use reusable components and consistent naming conventions.

### Collaboration

The project owner is the creative decision-maker.

Work collaboratively. Do not make major branding changes without
presenting the reasoning first.

When presenting visual concepts, explain: - The creative idea - The
visual direction - Why it fits the brand - How it contributes to the
campaign

Do not assume that every suggestion is automatically approved.
